SECTION A - SET A1

Matrix Fundamentals and Core Theorems | 2025 worked solutions

2025 Q1(a) - Definitions

Define lower triangular, symmetric, nilpotent and idempotent matrices with examples.

Lower triangular: \(a_{ij}=0\) for \(i<j\). Example \(\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\2&3&0\\4&5&6\end{bmatrix}\).
Symmetric: \(A^T=A\). Example \(\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&3\end{bmatrix}\).
Nilpotent: \(A^m=0\) for some positive integer \(m\). Example \(N=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\), \(N^2=0\).
Idempotent: \(A^2=A\). Example \(P=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{bmatrix}\).

2025 Q1(b) - Idempotent test

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}-1&3&5\\1&-3&-5\\-1&3&5\end{bmatrix}\). Test whether \(A\) is idempotent.

Direct multiplication gives \[A^2=\begin{bmatrix}-1&3&5\\1&-3&-5\\-1&3&5\end{bmatrix}=A.\]

\(A^2=A\), therefore \(A\) is idempotent.

2025 Q1(c) - Symmetric + skew-symmetric decomposition

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}3&1&0\\5&0&3\\2&4&7\end{bmatrix}\).

Use the unique decomposition \[P=\frac12(A+A^T),\qquad Q=\frac12(A-A^T),\qquad A=P+Q.\] Hence \[P=\begin{bmatrix}3&3&1\\3&0&7/2\\1&7/2&7\end{bmatrix},\qquad Q=\begin{bmatrix}0&-2&-1\\2&0&-1/2\\1&1/2&0\end{bmatrix}.\] Clearly \(P^T=P\) and \(Q^T=-Q\).

2025 Q1(d) - Solve by matrix method

\(4x-5y+z=2\), \(3x+y-2z=9\), \(x+4y+z=5\).

With \(AX=B\), \(\det A=72\ne0\), so the solution is unique. Row reduction (or \(X=A^{-1}B\)) gives

\(x=2,\quad y=1,\quad z=-1\).

SECTION A - SET A2

Inverse, Rank, Consistency and Linear Dependence | 2025 worked solutions

2025 Q2(a) - Inverse by elementary transformation

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&20&2\\2&-1&3\\4&1&8\end{bmatrix}\).

\(\det A=-79\ne0\), so the inverse exists. Gauss-Jordan reduction of \([A\mid I]\) gives \[A^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}11/79&2&-62/79\\4/79&0&-1/79\\-6/79&-1&41/79\end{bmatrix}.\] Check: multiplying \(AA^{-1}\) gives \(I_3\).

2025 Q2(b) - Parameterized system

Classify \(3x-2y+z=b\), \(5x-8y+9z=3\), \(2x+y+az=-1\).

The coefficient determinant is \[\det A=-14(a+3).\] Therefore \(a\ne-3\) gives a unique solution for every \(b\). For \(a=-3\), the coefficient matrix has rank \(2\). A left-null relation among the equations is \(-3R_1+R_2+2R_3=0\); consistency therefore requires the same relation on the constants: \[-3b+3+2(-1)=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad b=\frac13.\]

Unique: \(a\ne-3\) (any \(b\)). Infinite solutions: \(a=-3,\ b=1/3\). No solution: \(a=-3,\ b\ne1/3\).

2025 Q2(c) - Linear independence

\(X_1=(1,2,4)\), \(X_2=(2,-1,3)\), \(X_3=(0,1,2)\).

Place the vectors as columns: \[M=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&0\\2&-1&1\\4&3&2\end{bmatrix},\qquad \det M=-5\ne0.\]

The three vectors are linearly independent.

SECTION A - SET A3

Echelon/Canonical/Normal Form and Eigen Analysis | 2025 worked solutions

2025 Q3(a) - Rank and forms

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}2&-6&-2&-3\\5&-13&-4&-7\\-1&4&1&2\\0&1&0&1\end{bmatrix}\).

Elementary row operations reduce \(A\) to \[\operatorname{RREF}(A)=I_4.\] Hence all four columns are pivot columns. The row-canonical form and normal form are both \(I_4\).

\(\operatorname{rank}(A)=4\); \(A\) is non-singular.

2025 Q3(b) - Eigenvalues/eigenvectors

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}6&-2&2\\-2&3&-1\\2&-1&3\end{bmatrix}\).

The characteristic polynomial factors as \[\det(A-\lambda I)=(8-\lambda)(2-\lambda)^2.\] Thus \(\lambda=8\) and \(\lambda=2\) (double). A convenient eigenvector for \(\lambda=8\) is \((2,-1,1)^T\). For \(\lambda=2\), an eigenspace basis is \[(1,2,0)^T,\qquad (-1,0,2)^T.\]

SECTION A - SET A4

Vector Kinematics, Work and Cayley-Hamilton | 2025 worked solutions

2025 Q4(a) - Speed and acceleration

\(x=e^{-t}\), \(y=2\cos 3t\), \(z=2\sin 3t\). Find magnitudes at \(t=0\).

\[\mathbf v=(-e^{-t},-6\sin3t,6\cos3t),\qquad \mathbf a=(e^{-t},-18\cos3t,-18\sin3t).\] At \(t=0\), \(\mathbf v=(-1,0,6)\) and \(\mathbf a=(1,-18,0)\).

\(|\mathbf v(0)|=\sqrt{37}\), \(|\mathbf a(0)|=\sqrt{325}=5\sqrt{13}\).

2025 Q4(b) - Work

Displacement \(\mathbf r=3\mathbf i+\mathbf j-5\mathbf k\) and constant force \(\mathbf F=2\mathbf i-\mathbf j-\mathbf k\).

For a constant force, \(W=\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf r=2(3)+(-1)(1)+(-1)(-5)=10\).

\(W=10\) units.

2025 Q4(c) - Cayley-Hamilton and inverse

\(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}\).

The characteristic equation is \(\lambda^2-2\lambda+3=0\). By Cayley-Hamilton, \[A^2-2A+3I=0.\] Since \(\det A=3\ne0\), multiply by \(A^{-1}\): \[A-2I+3A^{-1}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad A^{-1}=\frac{2I-A}{3}.\]

\(A^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}1/3&-2/3\\1/3&1/3\end{bmatrix}\).

SECTION B - SET B5

Laplace Fundamentals, Direct Transform, Periodic and Unit Step | 2025 worked solutions

2025 Q5(a)(i) - Shift + multiplication by \(t\)

\(F(t)=(t+2)e^{-3t}\sin 5t\).

First, \(\mathcal L\{\sin5t\}=5/(s^2+25)\) and \[\mathcal L\{t\sin5t\}=-\frac{d}{ds}\frac{5}{s^2+25}=\frac{10s}{(s^2+25)^2}.\] Therefore \(\mathcal L\{(t+2)\sin5t\}=10s/(s^2+25)^2+10/(s^2+25)\). The factor \(e^{-3t}\) shifts \(s\mapsto s+3\):

\(\displaystyle \mathcal L\{F\}=\frac{10(s+3)}{(((s+3)^2+25)^2)}+\frac{10}{(s+3)^2+25}\).

2025 Q5(a)(ii) - Periodic function

\(G(t)=t\) for \(0<t<2\), \(G(t)=0\) for \(2<t<4\), \(G(t+4)=G(t)\).

For period \(T=4\), \[\mathcal L\{G\}=\frac{1}{1-e^{-4s}}\int_0^2 t e^{-st}\,dt.\] Integration by parts gives \[\int_0^2te^{-st}dt=\frac1{s^2}-e^{-2s}\left(\frac2s+\frac1{s^2}\right).\] Thus

\(\displaystyle \mathcal L\{G\}=\frac{s^{-2}-e^{-2s}(2s^{-1}+s^{-2})}{1-e^{-4s}}\).

2025 Q5(b) - Unit-step load

\(p(t)=5t\) for \(0<t<4\) and \(20\) for \(t\ge 4\).

Use the jump rule: \[p(t)=5t+[20-5t]u(t-4)=5t-5(t-4)u(t-4).\] Hence

\(\displaystyle P(s)=\frac{5}{s^2}-\frac{5e^{-4s}}{s^2}=\frac{5(1-e^{-4s})}{s^2}\).

2025 Q5(c)(i) - Delayed inverse transform

\(f(s)=e^{-5s}/(s-2)^4\).

Without the delay, \[\mathcal L^{-1}\{(s-2)^{-4}\}=e^{2t}\frac{t^3}{3!}.\] Applying the second shift:

\(\displaystyle f(t)=u(t-5)e^{2(t-5)}\frac{(t-5)^3}{6}\).

2025 Q5(c)(ii) - Partial fractions

\(g(s)=\dfrac{2s^2-4}{(s+1)(s-2)(s-3)}\).

\[g(s)=-\frac{1}{6(s+1)}-\frac{4}{3(s-2)}+\frac{7}{2(s-3)}.\]

\(\displaystyle g(t)=-\frac16e^{-t}-\frac43e^{2t}+\frac72e^{3t}\).

SECTION B - SET B6

Inverse Laplace, Convolution, ODE and Applications | 2025 worked solutions

2025 Q6(a) - Convolution

\(\mathcal L^{-1}\{1/[(s+2)^2(s-2)]\}\).

Take \(F(s)=1/(s+2)^2\) and \(G(s)=1/(s-2)\), so \(f(t)=te^{-2t}\) and \(g(t)=e^{2t}\). Then \[(f*g)(t)=\int_0^t \tau e^{-2\tau}e^{2(t-\tau)}d\tau.\] Evaluating the elementary integral and simplifying gives

\(\displaystyle \frac{e^{2t}}{16}-\frac{e^{-2t}}{16}-\frac{t e^{-2t}}{4}\).

2025 Q6(b) - Earthquake response ODE

\(x''+2x'+x=e^{-t}\), \(x(0)=0\), \(x'(0)=1\).

Because the differential operator is \((D+1)^2\), put \(x=e^{-t}y\). Then \((D+1)^2x=e^{-t}y''\), so \(y''=1\). Hence \[y=\frac{t^2}{2}+C_1t+C_2.\] Initial conditions give \(C_2=0\) and \(C_1=1\).

\(\displaystyle x(t)=e^{-t}\left(t+\frac{t^2}{2}\right)\).

SECTION B - SET B7

Vector Differential Calculus | 2025 worked solutions

2025 Q6(c) - Temperature gradient

\(T=x^2ye^{-z}+3xy^2+z^3\). At \((1,2,0)\) find magnitude and direction of maximum increase.

\[\nabla T=(2xye^{-z}+3y^2,\ x^2e^{-z}+6xy,\ -x^2ye^{-z}+3z^2).\] At \((1,2,0)\), \(\nabla T=(16,13,-2)\). The maximum directional derivative is \(|\nabla T|\).

\(\displaystyle |\nabla T|=\sqrt{429}\), direction \(=(16,13,-2)/\sqrt{429}\).

2025 Q7(a) - Expansion/compression

For \(\mathbf V=(x^2y+yz)\mathbf i+(xy^2+xz)\mathbf j+(yz^2+x^2z)\mathbf k\), determine at \((1,2,1)\).

\[\nabla\cdot\mathbf V=2xy+2xy+(2yz+x^2).\] At \((1,2,1)\) this is \(4+4+5=13>0\).

Divergence \(=13\); the airflow is locally expanding (source-like).

2025 Q7(b) - Irrotational field and potential

\(\mathbf V=(x^3+yz)\mathbf i+(y^3+xz)\mathbf j+(z^3+xy)\mathbf k\).

All three curl components cancel, so \(\nabla\times\mathbf V=0\). Integrate \(\phi_x=x^3+yz\): \[\phi=\frac{x^4}{4}+xyz+g(y,z).\] Matching \(\phi_y\) and \(\phi_z\) gives

\(\displaystyle \phi=\frac{x^4+y^4+z^4}{4}+xyz+C\).

2025 Q7(c) - Conservative field and work

\(\mathbf F=(2xy+z^2)\mathbf i+(x^2+2yz)\mathbf j+(2xz+y^2)\mathbf k\).

A potential is \[\phi=x^2y+y^2z+xz^2,\] because \(\nabla\phi=\mathbf F\). Therefore work is path independent: \[W=\phi(2,1,1)-\phi(1,0,0)=7-0.\]

\(W=7\).

SECTION B - SET B8

Line, Surface and Volume Integration | 2025 worked solutions

2025 Q8(a) - Surface flux

\(\mathbf A=18z\mathbf i-12\mathbf j+3y\mathbf k\) over \(2x+3y+6z=12\) in the first octant.

Write \(z=(12-2x-3y)/6\). Taking the normal with positive \(z\) component, the vector area element is \[(-z_x,-z_y,1)\,dxdy=(1/3,1/2,1)\,dxdy.\] Then \(\mathbf A\cdot d\mathbf S=(6z-6+3y)dxdy=(6-2x)dxdy\). The projection is \(0\le x\le6\), \(0\le y\le(12-2x)/3\). Hence \[\iint_S\mathbf A\cdot\hat n\,dS=\int_0^6\int_0^{(12-2x)/3}(6-2x)\,dy\,dx=24.\]

Flux \(=24\) for the upward/positive normal.

2025 Q8(b) - Vector volume integral

\(\mathbf F=2xz\mathbf i-x\mathbf j+y^2\mathbf k\), bounded by \(x=0\), \(y=0\), \(y=6\), \(z=x^2\), \(z=4\).

The bounds are \(0\le x\le2\), \(0\le y\le6\), \(x^2\le z\le4\). Integrate each component: \[\iiint 2xz\,dV=128,\qquad \iiint(-x)\,dV=-24,\qquad \iiint y^2\,dV=384.\]

\(\displaystyle \iiint_V\mathbf F\,dV=128\mathbf i-24\mathbf j+384\mathbf k\).

2025 Q8(c) - Work around a circle

\(\mathbf F=(2x-y+z)\mathbf i+(x+y-z^2)\mathbf j+(3x-2y+4z)\mathbf k\), circle \(x^2+y^2=9\) in the \(xy\)-plane.

On \(z=0\), \(P=2x-y\), \(Q=x+y\). For counter-clockwise orientation, Green’s theorem gives \[\oint_C Pdx+Qdy=\iint_R\left(Q_x-P_y\right)dA=\iint_R(1-(-1))dA=2(9\pi).\]

\(W=18\pi\) (counter-clockwise); reverse orientation changes the sign.
